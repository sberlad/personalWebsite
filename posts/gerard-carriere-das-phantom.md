Alberich is not real either. The title needs that admission at the beginning.

What changes with Gérard Carrière is the kind of distance available to me. Myth can make a character's emotional life feel larger than ordinary behavior. A man inside a story about an opera house gives me fewer excuses for speaking as though every thought were carved in stone.

### First, which Phantom?

<a href="https://harztheater.de/produktion/das-phantom" target="_blank" rel="noopener">I sing Gérard Carrière in Harztheater’s *Das Phantom*</a>: <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maury_Yeston" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Maury Yeston</a> and <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Kopit" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Arthur Kopit</a>’s musical, in Wolfgang Adenberg’s German translation. It is a different adaptation from <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Lloyd_Webber" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Andrew Lloyd Webber</a>’s musical.

That distinction matters artistically as well as practically. I have to meet the character in the piece I am singing, rather than import the atmosphere of another version because its title is more familiar.

With Carrière, I am drawn to the relationship between care and responsibility. A man can believe he is protecting someone while also protecting himself from a truth he does not want spoken. Tenderness and avoidance can occupy the same gesture.

### A sentence before a symbol

The temptation with paternal roles is to arrive carrying their meaning: the father, the protector, the man with a past. Those are descriptions an audience might make afterward. They are not especially playable actions.

I need something smaller and more immediate. Am I trying to reassure somebody? Prevent a question? Gain another moment before saying what must be said? A scene begins to breathe when I give the other person something they can resist.

This is where musical theatre can unsettle an opera singer productively. I cannot assume that the generosity of the sound will make the exchange credible. The words must feel addressed to a particular person, even when the music opens them into a larger emotional space.

### Intimacy still needs technique

Singing conversationally does not mean neglecting the line. A supposedly natural delivery can become another effect if I impose it everywhere. Yeston's music asks for its own scale, and that scale can expand.

My job is to let the expansion come from the situation. I want a phrase to grow because something can no longer remain contained, rather than because I have reached the part where a singer is expected to show feeling.

Carrière is fictional. So are many of the people whose lives I borrow onstage. The test is whether I allow him an ordinary human difficulty: wanting to do right while knowing that his own choices have helped make right so difficult.
