---

Returning to a character sounds reassuring until you realize that memory has been rehearsing him without you.

I remember the physical sensation of an entrance, the shape of an argument, the emotional certainty that made a scene possible. Three years later, those memories may be useful. They may also have become a collection of answers to questions I am no longer asking.

Looking from Das Rheingold in 2024 toward Siegfried in 2027, I want to meet Alberich again without simply restoring an earlier performance.

### The same man, a different part

<a href="https://harztheater.de/produktion/siegfried" target="_blank" rel="noopener">In 2027 I return to Alberich in *Siegfried* at Harztheater</a>, continuing the character’s story at the same house. That continuity matters. So does the change: Siegfried gives Alberich different music and a different dramatic situation. I am returning to the man through a new part.

In *Rheingold*, we witness the experiences that drive him toward the ring and its curse. <a href="https://www.lnob.lv/en/production/siegfried/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">In *Siegfried*'s story</a>, he remains caught in the struggle for a power he no longer possesses. The immediate wound has acquired a history.

How much of the earlier man should remain audible? Enough that his persistence has a human source. But if every phrase carries the full heat of the original humiliation, I leave no room for what waiting has done to him.

### Experience can simplify, or harden

I hope that the intervening work allows me to do less. Not less thinking, but fewer unnecessary demonstrations of it. A character can become clearer when I stop illustrating everything I understand about him.

Yet experience can harden a choice as readily as it can refine one. “This worked before” is useful evidence, not a final instruction. I need to listen to the new score, the new encounter and the colleagues in front of me.

The voice will also arrive with its own present condition. I cannot prepare a role on the assumption that the instrument owes me the sensations I remember from 2024.

### What continuity should preserve

[In my first Alberich essay](blog.html#post-becoming-wagners-alberich), I explored the relationship between humiliation, power and restraint. Those questions remain. Their proportions may change.

Perhaps that is what returning can teach me: fidelity to a character does not require loyalty to every old gesture. It requires remembering what is at stake while allowing new evidence to alter how I show it.

Alberich has spent the years holding on. I would like the singer to learn when to let go.
