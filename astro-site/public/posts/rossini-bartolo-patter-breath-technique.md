---

The title is a provocation. I cannot prove that comedy is harder than every other kind of singing. I can say that it is exceptionally good at revealing the work I have not done.

In preparing Dr. Bartolo for <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gioachino_Rossini" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Rossini</a>'s *Il barbiere di Siviglia*, speed is the obvious challenge. It is also the least useful place to begin. If I make speed the goal, the text becomes an obstacle course and breath becomes whatever I can steal between obstacles.

Bartolo would like you to know he is a man of authority. Rossini keeps testing how long that authority can survive contact with other people.

### The words have to belong to a thought

<a href="https://harztheater.de/produktion/il-barbiere-di-sivglia" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Harztheater's production page sets out Bartolo's place in the intrigue</a>: a guardian whose plans for Rosina meet more ingenuity than he can control. His verbal abundance is part of that struggle.

For me, patter begins with a sentence I understand at speaking speed. Where is the accusation? What is evidence, and what is merely a man enjoying the sound of his own expertise? Which word changes the direction of the argument?

Those distinctions organize the passage. If every syllable receives equal importance, I spend energy without making the thought clearer. The audience may hear a feat of articulation while losing the person who needs to say it.

### Breathing before the emergency

My working approach is to establish the text and rhythm slowly, then bring the pitch and increasing tempo into that organization. The breath plan needs to be present early. Otherwise I can accidentally rehearse a pattern of running out and rescuing myself.

Consonants need to be precise enough to distinguish words without becoming heavy impacts. Vowels still carry the sung line. The challenge is to give each its job while keeping the mechanism available for the next phrase.

A useful checkpoint is whether a passage still has direction at a slower tempo. Speed can conceal a muddled intention just as readily as it can expose untidy diction.

### Control is the joke

Bartolo cannot know that he is losing a Rossini comedy. He believes his precautions are reasonable and his indignation justified. The more exact his argument becomes, the more revealing its collapse can be.

That is why I want the technical preparation to disappear into the character's effort. He is not demonstrating breath management. He is trying, with increasing desperation, to finish a sentence that will finally put everybody in their place.

Comedy asks me to make that struggle clear while the musical clock keeps running. Rossini supplies the clock. I have to supply the man who thinks he can control it.
