---

A guest contract has a clear dramatic shape: arrive, prove yourself, make the piece, leave. An ensemble position asks what you are like after everybody has already met you.

That is a less glamorous question, and perhaps a more revealing one.

I joined the Harztheater ensemble in 2022, after years of work that included guest engagements in Israel and Germany. [My biography traces that path](about.html). The change was more than an address or a longer contract. It changed the timescale on which I could understand my own work.

### What a guest learns quickly

Guest work teaches a particular alertness. You enter a rehearsal room with customs you do not yet know. You listen for how the conductor communicates, how the director uses time, what your colleagues need from you. You have to be prepared enough to remain available.

There is freedom in that temporary intensity. A project can have its own atmosphere. You encounter different musical assumptions and discover that something you thought was a universal rule is simply how the last house worked.

But each arrival also consumes energy. You rebuild trust, learn practical systems and establish who you are. Even when the artistic work goes well, the next beginning remains part of the job.

### What continuity makes visible

In an ensemble, colleagues hear more than the performance you would choose as your introduction. They hear you learning. They see the role that arrives easily and the one that takes longer. Familiarity can feel exposing because the polished account of yourself has less room to hide.

It can also make collaboration more exact. A shared musical reference does not need explaining from the beginning. You learn how somebody prepares an entrance or signals that a scene needs more space. Trust becomes a practical resource.

The risk is habit. Knowing one another can turn into assuming one another. A familiar room still deserves active listening.

### Stability needs boundaries too

An ensemble contract does not remove the need to think carefully about workload, repertoire or recovery. Variety can develop an artist; it can also tempt an artist to confuse adaptability with unlimited availability. I need to know what the voice can do repeatedly, inside a real season.

I value both ways of working. The freelance circuit asks how well I can enter a new artistic world. Ensemble life asks how much I can contribute to a world that continues tomorrow.

Belonging has artistic consequences. So does remembering that belonging is something we practice, not something a contract completes.
