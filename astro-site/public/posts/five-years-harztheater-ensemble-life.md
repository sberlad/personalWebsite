I joined Harztheater in September 2022. This coming September will make five years. That is long enough to recognize patterns in my work, and not quite long enough to trust every conclusion I draw from them.

The first things an ensemble teaches are visible: roles, schedules, musical styles, the practical geography of a house. The slower lessons concern what other people can depend on me to bring into the room.

### Preparation becomes a relationship

At the beginning of a career, preparation can feel like private insurance. If I know the part thoroughly enough, perhaps nobody will discover the things I do not yet know.

Repeated work with the same colleagues changes that idea. Preparation makes time available to other people. It lets a rehearsal move beyond correcting notes and toward testing an interpretation. It gives me enough security to hear a proposal that disrupts my plan.

I still want to arrive ready. I understand readiness less as protection and more as an offer.

### Range does not mean having no center

My work here has crossed opera, operetta and musical theatre. <a href="https://harztheater.de/theater/ensemble" target="_blank" rel="noopener">The theatre's ensemble biography records roles from Wagner and Dvořák to *Blues Brothers* and the 2026/27 repertoire</a>. Variety is exciting, but the number of different things on a list is not itself evidence of growth.

The better question is what transfers. Can the rhythmic attention demanded by one piece clarify another? Can an experience of restraint help when the next role invites force? Can I recognize the moment when an old solution is arriving out of habit?

Versatility needs a stable center: an instrument I understand, a willingness to listen and enough judgment to distinguish a productive challenge from an unsuitable demand.

### Trust includes the unremarkable days

An ensemble relationship is built partly in performances people remember. It is also built in rehearsals nobody will write about. Arriving prepared, responding clearly, making room for a colleague and staying attentive after my own difficult passage are small acts with cumulative effects.

Familiarity does not guarantee generosity. It can produce impatience as easily as trust. The ongoing work is to keep encountering people rather than merely recognizing them.

In [my earlier comparison of ensemble and freelance life](blog.html#post-opera-ensemble-life-freelance-career), I asked what it means to belong to a theatre that continues tomorrow. Approaching five years, the question feels more concrete. Tomorrow has become a long series of actual rooms, people and shared tasks.

I still want to sing well. I also want my way of working to help other people sing, play and act well. That is a less photogenic ambition than a new role. It is one I can keep practicing after the anniversary has passed.
