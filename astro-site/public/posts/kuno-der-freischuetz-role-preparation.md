---

A father's blessing sounds generous. It also implies that he has the power to withhold it.

That is the tension I want to keep alive while preparing Kuno in <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Maria_von_Weber" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Weber</a>'s *Der Freischütz*. The role invites warmth and authority. I need to ask what happens when those qualities become difficult to distinguish from permission.

<a href="https://harztheater.de/produktion/der-freischuetz" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Harztheater's production is scheduled to premiere in Bernburg in March 2027, with me as Kuno</a>. Before the performance has happened, the useful work is to identify the questions rather than announce what the finished character will be.

### A respectable world can create dangerous pressure

The opera connects Max's future with Agathe to a shooting trial. Fear of failure helps draw him toward the supernatural bargain at the center of the story. The frightening forest is unforgettable, but it does not exist independently of the human expectations outside it.

Kuno belongs to the world that treats the test as meaningful. That does not require him to be malicious. A tradition can feel reassuring to the person explaining it and terrifying to the person whose life depends on passing it.

If I play him only as a kindly father, I risk making that pressure invisible. If I make him a tyrant from the outset, I lose the possibility that he sincerely believes the system protects something valuable.

### German Romantic opera needs ordinary speech too

The label “Romantic” can encourage a generalized atmosphere: forest, mystery, solemnity, a voice made suitably grand. But Kuno has specific things to communicate to specific people.

I want the German text to have the direction of a thought. An account of custom needs to persuade; a reassurance needs a listener. In the movement between speech and music, the character must remain one person rather than become alternately an actor explaining the plot and a singer providing authority.

The bass voice gives me gravity. It does not tell me whether a particular phrase offers comfort, expects obedience or quietly avoids somebody else's fear.

### Fathers are not interchangeable

Vodník fears the world his daughter wants to enter. Carrière carries the consequences of earlier choices. Kuno stands within a social order whose rules seem established. The resemblance among these roles helps me recognize a recurring question in my repertoire; the differences keep that question alive.

What does each man believe love entitles him to decide?

I do not expect Kuno to answer it neatly. I want his care to be audible, and the limits of his understanding to remain visible. A blessing becomes dramatically interesting when we can hear both the love inside it and the authority that makes it necessary.
